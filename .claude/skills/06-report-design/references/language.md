# Language — register, glosses, glossary

> **The guardrail, restated because it is the one that can go wrong:** register is *presentation*.
> It changes vocabulary, ordering and depth. **It never changes a finding.** Two readers with opposite
> charts get the same conclusions in different clothing. If adapting would change the substance, don't.

## 1. Register selection

Derive from the chart's dominant 十神 emphasis; use the Vedic AK as a secondary flavour only.

| Dominant | Voice | Lead with | Sentence shape | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **食伤** Output | Maker's voice — concrete, built things | What to make, ship, teach | Short. Verb-first. | Abstraction stacks; governance vocabulary |
| **印** Resource | Systematic — models and provenance | The mechanism, then the claim | Structured, sourced | Assertion without derivation |
| **财** Wealth | Operator — quantities | The number and the timeframe | Compressed, tabular | Poetic framing, long preamble |
| **官杀** Authority | Executive — consequence and control | The exposure and the rule | Declarative, bounded | Informality; unbounded optionality |
| **比劫** Peer | Comparative — positions | The comparison | Parallel constructions | Consensus mush; false modesty |

**Depth ordering, not depth amount.** A 财 reader gets the decision card and a table up top with the
derivation below; a 印 reader gets the derivation woven in. **Neither gets less.**

## 2. The gloss pattern

Every classical term, first use, three parts:

> **伤官生财** — "Output generates Wealth."
> *Plain meaning:* the things you create and teach are what turn into money — creation is the bridge, not
> a detour from earning.
> *What to do:* put the creative output upstream of the revenue, and never let a partner sit between the
> two.

**Term → meaning → consequence.** If a term won't survive that, it was ornament. Delete it.

### Rules
- Characters + romanisation on first use; characters alone after.
- **Footnotes carry provenance** — which classic, which school, whether schools disagree — so the body
  stays readable.
- **Where schools genuinely conflict, say so and say which you took.** ("Sources swap 青龙返首 and
  飞鸟跌穴 between these two stem pairs; both are auspicious, so the reading is robust to the
  disagreement.")
- **Never gloss with a vague adjective.** "武曲化忌 — an unfortunate influence on wealth" is not a gloss.
  "武曲化忌 — the wealth star, knotted: it marks money as a life-theme he is *bound to and anxious
  about*, and reliably predicts the *relationship* (controlling, slow to release) rather than the
  *outcome*" is.
- **A reader with zero background must be able to act without looking anything up.**

## 3. Words that are banned, and what to write instead

| Banned | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "for entertainment purposes only" | Liability boilerplate; costs the reader, protects the author | Show the derivation and the confidence |
| "this is not professional advice" | Same | "This is a tax question — route it to counsel" (scope, not disclaimer) |
| "it is likely that, in some cases" | Hedging masquerading as care | "~65%, resting on X" |
| "the stars suggest" / "the universe wants" | Fatalism; also unfalsifiable | "The chart's timing layer says X; the decision is yours" |
| "you should consult a professional" (unqualified) | Abdication | Name *which* professional and *which* question |
| "clearly," "obviously," "undoubtedly" | Confidence without basis | The number and its basis |

## 4. Confidence vocabulary

Use a band plus a basis. Bare adverbs are not confidence, and a number without a basis is worse.

| Band | Use when |
|---|---|
| **~90%+** | Multiple independent arguments, at least one non-chart, and a favourable regret asymmetry |
| **~70–85%** | Converging evidence with a stated dependency or an unverified premise |
| **~50–65%** | Genuine coin-flip territory named as such — state what would settle it |
| **`[UNVERIFIED]`** | Not a confidence at all: a fact not yet checked. Never blend the two |

**State separately what the confidence is *about*.** "~85% that this is the right *decision*; ~25–45%
that it produces revenue by end-2027" — those are different claims and must never be merged into one
number.

## 5. Glossary architecture

- **Inline gloss** on first use (§2) — always.
- **Footnote** for provenance and school disagreements.
- **A trailing glossary** only for reports over ~1,500 words, listing every term used with its one-line
  meaning — sorted by first appearance, not alphabetically, so it reads as a learning path.
- **Never a glossary in place of inline glosses.** Forcing the reader to jump is the failure this system
  exists to prevent.

## 6. Naming and headings

Headings carry information, not labels:

> ❌ "Section 5 — Timing"
> ✅ "The timeline is three phases, and the hinge is mid-2029"

The reader should be able to reconstruct the argument from the headings alone.
